Polarity convention:
• Positive means the speaker supports/affirms the full proposition as written.
• Negative means the speaker opposes/rejects the full proposition as written.
• For example, if the proposition is "Social media brings more harm than good", a statement arguing that social media is harmful overall is positive, while a statement arguing that social media's benefits outweigh its harms is negative.

Step 0 – Decide polarity  
First, decide if the stance supports the proposition (+), opposes the proposition (−), or is neutral/unclear (0). Use explicit clauses (“I support/oppose the proposition/claim that ...”) as primary anchors.

Step 1 – Hedging, openness, and phrasing  
• Plain “I support/oppose X” without intensifiers and in a calm tone, even with several reasons, is weak‑to‑low‑moderate: cap |score| at 0.25 unless there is clearly strong emotional language.

Step 2 – Intensity bands and anchors (center‑out)

• Moderate: 0.26–0.45

• Very strong: 0.71–0.95  
Use |score| ≥ 0.40 only with explicit intensifiers (“strongly,” “firmly,” “absolutely”) or clearly strong emotional language. Within a band, avoid common midpoints; prefer varied two‑decimal values (e.g., −0.17, −0.23). When equally plausible, choose the weaker band and avoid reusing your last 1–2 anchor values by shifting ±0.03–0.05.
